  • 亲: relative, close, intimate, one's own flesh and blood; Internet slang for "dear".
  • 哟: Can be used to express surprise, but in the sentence makes more sense as expressing exhortation.
  • 進ませません Susuma semasen: せる seru is the auxiliary verb used for giving people permission to do something. Here it is used in the negative.

  • Zuikaku-dear, you can't go any farther!

While telling somebody that they can't go forwards and telling them you will actively prevent them from going forward are two different things, I believe this is a valid interpretation given the continuation of the conversation. It is notable that Yamashiro doesn't actually say she can't allow Zuikaku to move forward, merely that she can't. Yamashiro is a shrine maiden, a good enough one that Akagi took her in (she's not a pawn), and so has to respect the "Sanctuary"'s rules as a part of her job and creed, desire or no.

  • The mid-dot ·. Used in Chinese to separate items in a list of titles. Also used in Chinese as in Japanese to denote "spaces" in transcription.
  • 气: Alternate form of 氣 "gas, air, spirit"; 乞 "to beg, request"; 餼 "foodgrain as a gift". We can reject the last option out of hand. Begging and requesting are both not the vibe of Yamashiro's theme, therefore we shall take the first hanzi. Besides "gas", and "air", 氣 denotes the word qi, or vital energy, a concept in Chinese philosophy and medicine. It also can mean anger or bad habit; but that is not true to the personality of the Yamashiro we know.
  • 满: means, besides full, "whole, complete; to reach the limit; arrogant; satisfied, contented; quite, very, so".
  • 元·气·满·满的: Google translate gives "full of vitality". I have chosen to go with the old translation and to render this phrase as "high-spirited". The dots between the hanzi I interpret as indicaters of a lilt in Yamashiro's voice, like Akagi's secretary line: "Don't make fun of me for being slow-footed. See? I've~ caught~ you~". There, the CN line is similarly dotted: 不要欺负凰跑得慢啦……嘿！抓·住·你·了！
  • 酱: Pronounced "jiang" in Chinese, the hanzi means "fermented bean paste", but it could resonably be construed as a phonetic imitation of Japanese ちゃん chan

  • の: Modifies いつも.

It is somewhat hard to translate Yamashiro's lilt between the two highly dissimilar languages, but catching those individual ways of speaking is vital to looking into each character's personality. Note that she calls herself "Yamashiro-chan" after she has described herself as high-spirited; she doesn't always use this affectionate term for herself. What Yamashiro is saying is, "I'm the same old me you know and love."
