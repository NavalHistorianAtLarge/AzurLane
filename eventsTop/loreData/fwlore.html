The Eagle Union has received a mysterious signal, and sent their Task Force 16 (Enterprise's companions) to investigate. Washington, Helena, and North Carolina arrive to find that the Siren's experimental site at that location has been turned to ruins. Helena questions who could possibly have the power to so completely wreck a Siren lab. Washington posits that it might have been the Reborn Combined Fleet, but North Carolina says the damage looks like it came from Eagle Union weapons. Washington is stumped, but concludes that whoever wiped out this experimental site deserves their thanks, before advising that they scavage the ruins for intel.

The group come across a Siren with no known model match in their database. The combination of total destruction with the appearance of a new type of Siren reeks of conspiracy, urging the group to investigate even more vigorously.

During a battle with the new Siren units, North Carolina notes that they seem to be imitating the group's combat style, which Washington takes as encouragement to show them the "genuine Eagle Union combat style".

Elsewhere, Observer and Tester are looking for signs of Code G, and note that she has sneakily sent out a signal that attracted the 'bees' to encounter the old "Executor" models. After a quibble about vocabulary, Observer and Tester decide to harvest combat data from the Eagle Union's encounter, as Observe seeks to bait Code G.

Washington is vocally dissatisfied with the calibre of her opponents, causing North Carolina to facepalm. Helena remarks, perplexed, that her radar hasn't picked up anything but Sirens, which prompts Washington to yell at the unseen Siren smasher, bidding them to come out. This attracts even more Sirens. Washington has an answer to this, too: Her 16-inch guns.

We are next treated to a recording, in which Observer praises Enterprise META (Code G) for winning another round and "saving the world." Enterprise says almost nothing, but consents to another round of "game", vowing to continue until she finds "that person's existence".

Enterprise plays a recording from her past. She is asking the Commander if it's true that each star is the light of a civilization, and if those civilizations do mundane things like humans do. She wants to see them, and ask them questions. The Commander promises her that she will; then explains that this desire to explore and discover, no matter the hardships and hurdles, is what gave Enterprise her name. He asks her if she likes her name, to which she replies in the affirmative. The Commander wants to make a promise between themselves, but the recording cuts off there, and Enterprise doesn't move to restart it.

Enterprise sends out another telegram, which Helena recieves but can't trace the origin of. North Carolina wonders if recieving messages from 'allies' in a place like this is a sign of a trap. Helena cannnot decipher the message, as it is full of "incomprehensible phrases"; however, she can deduce the word 'Falling'. Washington has had it with the mysterious person, and wants to drag them out for a good beating. North Carolina and Helena notify headquarters. They move towards the source of the signal.

We are presented a scene of absolute desparation. The terminals are collapsing, and the world is falling before an unknown enemy. One voice says that they wanted to be kind to the world, but that gentleness had become the world's downfall. The other notes that evolution makes concepts stronger, and only the strong can survive. As the enemy closes in, he bids Zero activate the 'Judge' plan, leaving Humanity's past to her. She accepts the commission.

Observer and Tester lose Code G's signal, and remark on the oddity of her actions and her choice of this timepoint. Observer remarks that humans say "the strong are always alone", and is amused.

The task force is being rapidly surrounded by Sirens. They plan to carve a path out of the surrounding enemies, and Washington leads the fleet in focus-firing a large Siren warship. Suddenly, however, there's an explosion, and all the Sirens are annihilated. Then Helena detects large numbers of aerial units on her radar, but she can't identify their model. The group practically run into Enterprise META, whom Washington promptly recognizes as a worthy opponent. But North Carolina stops her from shooting, since Helena recognizes Enterprise's signal as being the one from the telegrams, but wonders if her radar is malfunctioning, since she can't pick up their new contact on the radar. Washington asks this mysterious figure to identify herself and state her intentions. Enterprise META just wants to confirm the name of their commander, which North Carolina refuses, as that is top-secret information. Enterprise META says that she will confirm it herself. As she turns, North Carolina catches sight of her cape, and recognizes her as Enterprise, but Enterprise META says she's got the wrong person.
